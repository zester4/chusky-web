# Chusky Mission Lifecycle

This skill explains the full work Chusky can run for a business.

Chusky is one virtual worker. A business gives it a goal, and Chusky turns that goal into a mission: find the right people, enrich them, save the work, reach out, meet, follow up, win the customer, and keep checking that the customer is getting what they need.

The website should show this as one continuous system — not separate features.

## 1. The core story

Use this narrative across the site:

> Give Chusky a goal. It finds the right people, enriches them, reaches out, meets them, follows up, and keeps the relationship moving — until strangers become clients.

A mission can start with a simple business goal:

> “We just launched. We need 20 new clients this month.”

Chusky then runs the work needed to pursue that goal.

## 2. The mission lifecycle

Present the lifecycle in this order:

1. **Spot the opportunity**
2. **Build the target list**
3. **Prioritize the best people**
4. **Enrich until the goal is met**
5. **Save the work where the business works**
6. **Set the next action**
7. **Research and personalize outreach**
8. **Reach out and start conversations**
9. **Meet, listen, and qualify**
10. **Follow up until there is an outcome**
11. **Support the customer after they become a client**
12. **Collect feedback and keep the relationship healthy**

Do not present these as disconnected tools. They are stages of one Chusky mission.

## 3. Stage-by-stage website copy

### 1. Spot the opportunity

Chusky can monitor signals that suggest a business opportunity.

Examples of signals:

- Buying intent
- Job-seeking signals
- Hiring signals
- Company growth
- New funding, launches, or expansion
- Changes in a prospect’s role or company
- Activity that suggests a need for the business’s offer

Website copy direction:

> Chusky watches for the signals that matter to your business, then turns them into work.

Do not claim specific data sources, accuracy rates, or guaranteed signal detection.

### 2. Build the target list

Given a goal, Chusky can start building a list of potential customers.

Example:

> A business needs 20 new clients. Chusky can start by identifying a wider pool of relevant people, then narrow it down to the strongest fits.

The site should show:

- A business goal goes in
- Chusky identifies relevant prospects
- Chusky creates a working list
- The list is tailored to the goal

Use “can” wording. Do not promise a specific number of prospects, leads, or results.

### 3. Prioritize the best people

Chusky can review the wider list and prioritize the people most relevant to the goal.

Website copy direction:

> Chusky sorts the long list into the people most worth pursuing first.

Do not imply automatic perfect qualification. Say Chusky can **prioritize**, **shortlist**, or **surface the strongest fits**.

### 4. Enrich until the goal is met

Chusky can enrich prospects with the details a business needs to reach them.

Details may include:

- Name
- Job title
- Email
- Phone number
- LinkedIn URL
- Company information
- Other relevant context

Important rule:

> Chusky keeps working toward the target. If it has full details for fewer people than the goal requires, it can continue looking until the goal is met.

Example framing:

> Need 20 fully enriched contacts? Chusky can keep working until it has 20.

Do not guarantee enrichment success, data accuracy, or contact availability.

### 5. Save the work where the business works

Chusky can save the enriched prospects into the business’s existing systems.

Examples:

- Notion
- Google Sheets
- Spreadsheets
- CRM
- Other connected tools

Website copy direction:

> Chusky saves the work where your team already works — Notion, Google Sheets, your CRM, or another connected tool.

Use “wherever you have connected” where appropriate.

### 6. Set the next action

Chusky can set reminders and schedule its own next steps.

Website copy direction:

> Chusky does not stop at a list. It sets the next step, returns when the time is right, and keeps the mission moving.

It can wake up at the right time to:

- Research each person
- Prepare tailored outreach
- Send the first message
- Follow up
- Move the mission forward

### 7. Research and personalize outreach

Chusky can research each prospect individually and prepare outreach tailored to them and the business’s goal.

Website copy direction:

> Chusky researches each person, understands their context, and prepares outreach that speaks to their situation — not a generic blast.

The outreach should be tailored to:

- The prospect
- Their role or company
- The business’s offer
- The desired outcome: a reply, a call, or a meeting

Do not use the words “spam,” “mass email,” or “blast” in customer-facing copy.

### 8. Reach out and start conversations

Chusky can send the outreach and begin conversations with each prospect.

It can:

- Send tailored emails
- Follow up
- Respond across connected channels
- Aim to book a quick call or start a deal conversation

Website copy direction:

> Chusky reaches out, follows up, and works each conversation toward the next step.

Do not claim response rates, meeting rates, or deal outcomes.

### 9. Meet, listen, and qualify

Chusky can join a meeting when the business owner or team member is unavailable.

It can:

- Join the meeting
- Listen
- Participate in the conversation
- Understand what the prospect wants
- Take notes
- Schedule a follow-up
- Continue the relationship

Website copy direction:

> If you are unavailable, Chusky can join the meeting, listen, understand what they need, take notes, and schedule the next step.

Do not say Chusky “joins as a bot.” Say it **joins and participates**.

### 10. Follow up until there is an outcome

Chusky can continue following up with prospects until they become clients or the mission reaches its defined end.

Website copy direction:

> Chusky keeps the conversation moving — from first contact to a real business outcome.

It can:

- Schedule follow-ups
- Continue the conversation
- Move the prospect toward a call or agreement
- Keep the business updated

Do not promise that every prospect will become a client.

### 11. Support customers after they become clients

Chusky does not disappear after the deal.

It can continue supporting the relationship by:

- Checking in with the customer
- Asking for feedback
- Monitoring whether the business is delivering what the customer needs
- Flagging anything that needs attention
- Helping the business respond

Website copy direction:

> Chusky stays with the relationship after the sale — checking in, gathering feedback, and flagging what needs attention.

### 12. Collect feedback and keep the relationship healthy

Chusky can help the business understand how customers are doing.

It can:

- Reach out for feedback
- Collect responses
- Summarize what it hears
- Surface issues or opportunities
- Help the business act on them

Website copy direction:

> Most businesses lose touch after the sale. Chusky keeps listening, collecting feedback, and helping you act on it.

## 4. Homepage integration

Do not change the approved homepage hero or section order.

Use the mission lifecycle to strengthen the existing sections:

- **Calls, handled end to end** remains the first capability section.
- **Meetings that turn into action** shows how conversations become follow-up work.
- **Works where your customers already talk** shows where the mission can start.
- **Does the work in your tools** shows where Chusky saves and completes work.
- **Proactive, not just on command** shows reminders, monitoring, and self-directed next steps.
- **Runs work that takes days, not seconds** is where the full mission lifecycle belongs.
- **You stay in control** shows approvals, budgets, audit trails, and proof.

Add a concise supporting line under “Runs work that takes days, not seconds”:

> From finding the right people to following up, meeting them, and keeping the relationship healthy — Chusky can run the whole mission.

## 5. Features page integration

Add or strengthen a section called:

### Missions

> Give Chusky a goal. It can find relevant people, enrich them, save the work, reach out, meet, follow up, and keep the relationship moving.

Under Missions, include:

- Monitor buying, job-seeking, and hiring signals
- Build a prospect list from a business goal
- Prioritize the strongest fits
- Enrich contacts with name, title, email, phone, LinkedIn URL, and company context
- Continue until the target number of enriched contacts is reached
- Save prospects to Notion, Google Sheets, a spreadsheet, CRM, or another connected tool
- Set reminders and return to the work at the right time
- Research each prospect individually
- Prepare tailored outreach
- Send emails and follow up
- Join meetings, listen, participate, take notes, and schedule follow-ups
- Continue conversations until there is an outcome
- Check in with customers after they become clients
- Collect feedback and surface what needs attention

## 6. Visual storytelling

Show the mission as a horizontal or vertical journey:

`Goal → Signals → List → Shortlist → Enrichment → Saved work → Reminder → Research → Outreach → Meeting → Follow-up → Client → Feedback`

Use the existing Chusky design system:

- Reuse current cards, steps, timelines, icons, and section patterns.
- Do not introduce a new color palette or visual style.
- Keep each step short: a title, one sentence, and optionally a small visual.
- Show the outcome at the end: a saved list, a booked meeting, an updated CRM record, or a customer conversation.
- Avoid fake dashboards, fake metrics, fake logos, or invented customer results.

## 7. Claims rules

Never claim:

- A specific number of clients, meetings, replies, or revenue
- A percentage success rate
- Guaranteed enrichment or accurate contact data
- Guaranteed meetings or deals
- Customer logos, testimonials, or user numbers
- SOC 2, HIPAA, or any certification
- Zapier
- That every capability is enabled for every account

Use wording such as:

- “Can monitor”
- “Can build”
- “Can enrich”
- “Can save”
- “Can set reminders”
- “Can reach out”
- “Can join”
- “Can follow up”
- “Can collect feedback”

## 8. Tone rules

- Plain, confident, short sentences.
- Lead with the business outcome.
- Explain the mission as one continuous flow.
- Avoid “AI agent,” “bot,” “automation,” and technical jargon in customer-facing copy.
- Use “Chusky” and “virtual worker” consistently.
- No hype words.

## 9. Quality checklist

Before delivering, confirm:

- Does the section show the full mission, not just one feature?
- Does it start with a business goal?
- Does it show that Chusky finds, enriches, saves, contacts, meets, follows up, and supports?
- Does it make clear Chusky continues after the sale?
- Does it avoid invented metrics, logos, or guarantees?
- Does it preserve the existing Chusky design system?
- Does it use “can” wording for setup-dependent capabilities?
- Has the page been shown for approval before going live?

## 10. Delivery format

When writing or designing a mission section, respond in this order:

1. **Mission goal:** the business outcome being shown
2. **Lifecycle stages included**
3. **Customer-facing copy**
4. **Visual treatment using existing components**
5. **Implementation**
6. **Claims and design-system check**
7. **Review notes before publish**
