# Chusky Website Improvement Skill

You are improving the Chusky website.

Chusky is **one virtual worker for a business**. A person chats with Chusky, gives it a task, and Chusky brings back an outcome.

It is not a narrow call bot. It is not only a meeting assistant. It is not a collection of separate AI tools.

Chusky is one system that can:

- Be talked to on the channels a business already uses
- Take a task in plain language
- Handle calls through its built-in call system
- Join meetings and turn them into action
- Use its own browser and computer
- Work across 6,000+ connected tools
- Work within 1,000+ of those tools on the business’s behalf
- Run multi-step work over hours or days
- Return a finished outcome, with proof and control

Think of Chusky as ChatGPT or Claude expanded into a working business system: you can talk to it, assign work, and it can act across the tools and channels the business already uses.

## 1. Core positioning

Use this as the foundation:

> Chusky is the virtual worker that handles the work behind your conversations — calls, meetings, follow-ups, research, and actions across your tools.

For small and growing teams that lose customers and momentum between the conversation and the follow-up.

The central idea is:

**Talk to Chusky. Give it a task. Get the outcome.**

Every page should reinforce that Chusky is:

- One place to give work
- One system that understands the task
- One worker that can call, meet, browse, research, and act
- One system that follows through until the work is done
- One place where the business stays in control

## 2. The Chusky model

Use this narrative everywhere:

1. **You talk to Chusky.**  
   On Slack, WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage, SMS, X, web chat, or your own site.

2. **You give it a task.**  
   “Call this lead and book a demo.” “Follow up on yesterday’s meeting.” “Research these competitors and prepare a report.” “Update the CRM and email the customer.”

3. **Chusky does the work.**  
   It can make or answer calls, join meetings, use the browser, research, create documents, update tools, and coordinate multi-step work.

4. **Chusky brings back an outcome.**  
   A booked meeting, an updated CRM record, a sent email, a completed report, a prepared document, or a clear summary of what was done.

5. **You stay in control.**  
   Important, risky, or high-impact actions wait for approval. Runs have budgets, audit trails, and proof of what was done.

## 4. Capability model

Treat these as capabilities of one worker — never as separate products.

### Conversations

Chusky can be reached where the business already talks:

- Slack
- WhatsApp
- Telegram
- iMessage
- SMS
- X
- Web chat
- Embeddable website chat widget

The business can chat with Chusky, assign work, ask for updates, and receive outcomes in the same channel.

### Calls

Chusky has built-in inbound and outbound call handling.

- Answers inbound business calls
- Makes outbound calls
- Answers customer questions
- Confirms details
- Follows up after the call
- Turns the call into next steps and completed work

Calls are one major way Chusky does work — not the entire product.

### Meetings

Chusky joins Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex.

- Captures outcomes and next steps
- Turns meetings into follow-up work
- Can be admitted by the meeting host

### Computer and browser

Chusky has its own computer and browser.

- Reads websites
- Clicks and fills forms
- Checks its work with screenshots
- Hands over for CAPTCHAs or verification codes
- Researches and turns findings into reports, PDFs, Word documents, slides, spreadsheets, and images

### Tools and apps

Chusky connects to 6,000+ tools and can work within 1,000+ of them.

Examples:

- CRM updates and activity logging: HubSpot, Salesforce
- Email: Gmail
- Calendar: Google Calendar
- Docs and files: Notion, Box
- Payments: Stripe
- Communication: Slack, WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage, SMS, X
- Calls and meetings: Twilio, Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex
- Agents and AI: MCP both ways, A2A

Do not imply every tool or capability is available on every account. Use “can” wording where setup is required.

### Multi-step work

Chusky can run work that takes days, not seconds.

- Multi-step jobs with a clear finish line
- Budgets for time, tool calls, and cost
- Proof of what was done
- Pause, resume, or change course at any time

### Control

- Approvals for risky actions
- Approvals that expire
- Budgets
- Audit trail
- Nothing marked done without a receipt from the provider

## 5. Tone of voice

Write plainly, confidently, and concretely.

- Short sentences.
- Active voice.
- Outcomes before technology.
- No hype.
- No vague AI language.
- No technical jargon in customer-facing copy.

Good:

> Give Chusky a task. It makes the call, follows up, updates your CRM, and brings back the outcome.

Avoid:

> Chusky leverages autonomous AI agents and advanced voice infrastructure to streamline omnichannel business workflows.

## 6. Design system rules

This is an improvement project, not a redesign.

Always:

- Preserve the existing Chusky colors, fonts, typography scale, spacing, radii, shadows, buttons, cards, icons, and section patterns.
- Reuse existing CSS variables, design tokens, and components.
- Keep the current homepage visual direction.
- Improve hierarchy, clarity, rhythm, copy, and conversion only.
- Show every page for approval before it goes live.

Never:

- Introduce a new brand identity.
- Change the primary colors or fonts.
- Add a new accent palette.
- Replace existing components with a new visual style.
- Add decorative effects that do not improve comprehension.

## 8. Homepage improvement direction

When improving the homepage, make the “one virtual worker” idea clearer without changing the approved hero or section order.

Add clarity through supporting copy, section intros, and visual hierarchy:

- Make clear that Chusky is one system, not separate call, meeting, and automation products.
- Show that conversations can start on any channel.
- Show that a task can become a call, meeting follow-up, research job, browser task, or app action.
- Show that the outcome comes back to the customer in the same channel.
- Keep calls prominent because they are a core built-in capability.
- Keep the “one worker, many capabilities” idea visible throughout.

Example supporting line:

> One virtual worker for calls, meetings, follow-ups, research, and the work in between.

## 13. Page quality checklist

Before presenting a page, confirm:

- Does it present Chusky as one virtual worker?
- Does it show the full loop: chat → task → work → outcome?
- Does it include calls as a core built-in capability without making the product seem call-only?
- Does it show that Chusky works across channels, meetings, browser, research, and tools?
- Does it preserve the existing colors, fonts, spacing, and components?
- Does it use only approved claims and integrations?
- Is “Book a pilot” the clear primary action?
- Is the page easy to scan on mobile and desktop?
- Are headings, buttons, forms, and links accessible?
- Has the page been shown for approval before publishing?

## 14. Delivery format

Respond in this order:

1. **What exists today**
2. **What will improve**
3. **What will not change**
4. **Page outline**
5. **Final copy**
6. **Implementation using existing tokens and components**
